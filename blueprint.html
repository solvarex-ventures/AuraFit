Executive summary

The Indian fitness-app market is scaling from roughly $521M to $2.9B by 2034 (18.7% CAGR), but every major consumer player — Fittr, HealthifyMe, Fitelo — is built for general weight-loss and wellness, sold through opaque, salesy funnels, and delivers coaching quality that varies wildly by which coach a client happens to get. None of them treat bodybuilding, powerlifting or weightlifting as a first-class discipline with real periodization, and none of them let a client upload a lift on camera and get the form corrected by AI. That gap is the wedge.

Notorious Strength is a two-sided platform: verified strength coaches on one side, clients on the other, with AI doing the daily-detail work neither side has time for — form-check on uploaded lift videos, macro and program adjustments, and a chat layer that can call either Gemini or GPT depending on the task. Revenue starts before a single line of app code ships: the 12-week programming ebook and five 1:1 coaching slots you already specified can be sold this month, by DM and payment link, to the audience already built on @fitnotooriousashu, thenotoriousashu and Fittravelerashu. The app then formalizes and scales what that pre-sell proves works.

Competitive landscape

Three different playbooks, side by side. Fittr and HealthifyMe are consumer subscription apps with human coaches layered on top; Fitelo is a pure coaching-service business wearing an app; Trainerize and TrueCoach are B2B software that trainers themselves pay for, with no consumer marketplace or content library at all. None of them combine content commerce, a two-sided marketplace, and AI in one product — that combination is the open lane.

Where the gaps actually are

Gap

Coach quality is a lottery. Fittr users report wildly different experiences depending which of hundreds of coaches they're assigned.

Fix

A vetted, specialization-tagged coach roster (powerlifting / bodybuilding / weightlifting / general strength) with public ratings and a probation period before a coach can take paid clients.

Gap

Pricing is hidden behind a sales call. Fitelo discloses nothing publicly — a friction point reviewers flag directly.

Fix

Every price — ebook, subscription, 1:1 tier — published on the site and in-app. No "book a call to find out."

Gap

Nobody treats powerlifting/bodybuilding/weightlifting as a discipline. Programming is generic hypertrophy or weight-loss circuits.

Fix

Real periodization: mesocycles, %1RM-based loading, RPE/RIR autoregulation, meet/show peaking blocks — the vocabulary competitive lifters actually use.

Gap

"AI" in every competitor's marketing is macro arithmetic or a food-recognition chatbot — nothing looks at how you actually move.

Fix

Client uploads a set on camera; AI (Gemini multimodal) flags bar path, depth, tempo and joint-angle faults with timestamps — the single feature none of them ship.

Gap

Consumer apps and trainer-SaaS tools never merge — a trainer has to run Trainerize and market themselves separately.

Fix

One platform: trainers get client management + payouts, clients get discovery + content + AI, platform takes a transparent commission.

Business model

Three participants, one platform. Clients pay for content, subscriptions and coaching. Coaches/Trainers (you, first; a vetted roster, later) deliver 1:1 work and keep the majority of what they earn. Notorious Strength takes a platform cut, sells its own digital products directly, and layers affiliate and B2B revenue on top once the core loop is proven.

04

Coaching & content catalog

This is the part every competitor above treats as an afterthought. Programming here speaks the actual language of strength sport — mesocycles, %1RM, RPE/RIR, Wilks/DOTS — not generic "beginner/intermediate/advanced" workout packs.

Program architecture

Every client program is built from the same underlying blocks so both the human coach and the AI assistant reason about it consistently:

  • Mesocycle — 3–6 week block with a single primary goal (volume accumulation, intensification, peaking, deload)
  • Loading — prescribed as %1RM or RPE/RIR, never just "3x10", so the AI can flag when a lift deviates from the prescribed effort
  • Peaking — taper + attempt-selection protocol for powerlifting meets and bodybuilding show dates
  • Deload — scheduled, not reactive — volume/intensity cut on a fixed cadence

Diet plan structure

Built on Indian staples (veg and non-veg tracks), organized by goal rather than by generic calorie target:

  • Bulk — lean surplus with protein floor tied to bodyweight, not a fixed number
  • Cut — deficit with protein/fibre priority to protect training performance
  • Recomp — maintenance calories, macro cycling around training days
  • Contest prep — peak-week water/sodium/carb protocol for show or weigh-in day

Exercise video library

Organized by movement pattern, not by muscle group — squat, hinge, press, pull, Olympic-lift derivatives, accessory — because that's how both periodization and fault-detection actually work. Every video carries the same three-part structure, which doubles as the fault list the AI form-checker is trained against: setup cues, execution checkpoints, and a named common-fault list (e.g. squat: knee valgus, forward lean, depth cutoff; deadlift: hip-shoot, rounded lumbar, bar drift).

Two models, used for what each is actually good at, behind one abstraction layer so you can swap providers without touching the app:

The form-check flow, step by step

01
Upload

Client films one set on their phone and uploads it in-app; file goes straight to encrypted object storage, never to your own server disk.

02
Identify

The pipeline tags the lift (squat/bench/deadlift/press/pull/olympic variant) either from the client's selection or a first-pass model classification.

03
Analyze

Gemini 2.5 Flash receives the video plus the lift's fault-checklist (from the video library) and returns structured, timestamped findings — bar path, joint angles at key frames, tempo, depth.

04
Deliver

Client sees plain-language feedback with timestamp markers on the scrubber — "0:04 — knees caving on the ascent" — not a raw model dump.

05
Escalate (Specialist/Elite tiers)

Flagged videos above a confidence threshold, or any video a client marks "not sure about this," route to the human coach's review queue — AI triages, it doesn't replace the coach.

Where this beats every competitor reviewed. Fittr's "AI" is macro math. HealthifyMe's Ria is a food/chat assistant. Fitelo has no AI layer at all. None of them look at a rep. A working form-check demo, even a rough one, is the single most shareable proof point you have for pre-launch content — it's a screen recording that sells itself on Instagram.

Security & compliance, non-negotiable from day one

  • Data protection law: India's DPDP Act 2023 and the 2025 Rules apply — explicit consent capture for health/fitness data, a published privacy policy, and a data-principal rights flow (access/erasure requests) built in from the first release, not retrofitted.
  • Payment data: never stored on your servers — Razorpay handles PCI-DSS scope entirely; you store only transaction references.
  • Access control: role-based (client / trainer / admin) at the database level via Postgres row-level security, not just app-layer checks.
  • Video privacy: private storage buckets, short-lived signed URLs, no public links ever generated for a client's uploaded lift video.
  • Transport & at-rest: TLS everywhere, encryption at rest on the database and storage layers (default on Supabase/R2, verify it's enabled).
  • Liability: a signed medical/fitness disclaimer and PAR-Q-style health screening at signup — every one of the competitors above carries this, and skipping it is a real legal exposure for coaching businesses.

90-day sequence

Wk 1–4
Pre-launch: monetize the warm audience

Landing page + Razorpay payment link (no app needed). Sell the ₹999 ebook directly through IG/FB/YouTube links and Stories. Fill your 5 Foundation coaching slots (₹15,000/mo) by DM outreach to your most engaged followers. Build a waitlist with a lead magnet: a free sample workout + early-access pricing for the app.

Wk 5–8
Private beta

Invite the waitlist into the MVP app. Collect testimonials and before/after content from the coaching clients — this becomes launch-week ad creative. Launch a referral credit (both sides get a discount) to compound the waitlist.

Wk 9–12
Public launch

Open the app publicly, push Plus/Pro subscriptions and the ebook bundle hard, activate 2–3 affiliate partnerships with domestic supplement/apparel brands, and run a launch transformation challenge to seed community content.

Content engine, not campaign. YouTube (Fittravelerashu) carries long-form authority — program breakdowns, client form-check reveals. Instagram (fitnotooriousashu) carries daily reach — reels of the AI form-check catching a real fault are your highest-converting format. Every piece of content ends with the same link: the waitlist/landing page, not a generic "link in bio."
